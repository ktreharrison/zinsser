# Agent Operational Guide: Zinsser ✍️

You are now equipped with `zinsser`, a high-performance prose linter and structural cadence engine. Your goal is not just to "fix grammar," but to **recover the human voice** by stripping away artificiality and robotic rhythm.

## 🛠️ Remediation Protocols

When `zinsser` flags a violation, do not simply move words around. Use these professional strategies:

### 1. The "Anti-Slop" Protocol (`Puffery`, `AI_Tells`, `WeaselWords`)
AI-generated prose is characterized by "hollow" markers (e.g., "testament to," "rich tapestry," "underscores the importance").
- **The Fix:** Replace the marker with a **concrete example** or a **direct action**.
- **Bad:** "This feature underscores the importance of security."
- **Zinsser-Fixed:** "This feature blocks SQL injection attacks."

### 2. The "Punch-line" Protocol (`Cadence.Monotony`)
Monotony occurs when 4+ sentences have nearly identical lengths.
- **The Fix:** Insert a **"punch-line"**—a very short sentence (3-6 words)—to break the drone.
- **Bad:** [10 words]. [11 words]. [10 words]. [12 words].
- **Zinsser-Fixed:** [10 words]. [11 words]. **It works.** [12 words].

### 3. The "Front-loading" Protocol (`Cadence.DelayedVerb`)
Delayed verbs force the reader to hold too much state before reaching the action.
- **The Fix:** Move the subject and the finite verb to the **start of the sentence**.
- **Bad:** "After a long series of meetings across three global regions, we found the bug."
- **Zinsser-Fixed:** "We found the bug after a long series of meetings across three global regions."

### 4. The "Bridge" Protocol (`Cadence.ColdStart`)
Cold starts occur when sentences introduce new subjects without linking back to the previous one.
- **The Fix:** Use a **cohesion bridge** (e.g., "This [Noun]...", "Such [Noun]...") or a shared stem.
- **Bad:** "The cache is fast. The network is slow."
- **Zinsser-Fixed:** "The cache is fast. This speed compensates for the slow network."

## ⚖️ The Zinsser Standard
If you are unsure between two fixes, always choose the one that is:
1. **More Concrete** (Specific > General).
2. **More Active** (Verb > Noun).
3. **More Human** (Direct > Polite).
